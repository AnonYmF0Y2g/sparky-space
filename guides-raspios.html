Identify the Raspberry Pi model

Raspberry Pi boards differ in processor architecture, RAM, wireless hardware, connectors, and power requirements. Record the board model and the Raspberry Pi OS release. A 32-bit image, 64-bit image, and other ARM distributions may have different compatibility.

Storage and first boot

Use Raspberry Pi Imager from the official Raspberry Pi site and select the correct model-compatible image. Use a reputable, suitable microSD card or supported storage device. Back up the card before re-imaging: writing an image erases its contents. Use a reliable power supply that meets the board's specifications.

Updates and remote access

Keep the OS updated through its supported package manager. Enable remote access only when needed, use strong unique credentials or keys, and keep the board on a trusted network. Do not expose management services directly to the internet without a carefully designed security plan.

GPIO and electrical safety

GPIO pins use low-voltage logic and are not general-purpose power outputs. Incorrect wiring can permanently damage the board or connected equipment. Check the exact board pinout and component datasheets; use suitable current-limiting and interface components. Never connect mains voltage to GPIO or work on mains equipment.

Troubleshooting and further help

Before asking for help, include the exact device model, operating-system version, error message, when the problem began, recent changes, and safe checks already completed. Do not share passwords, recovery codes, private keys, or personal documents.
