Start here: privacy foundations

These guides focus on personal safety, informed choices, and defensive privacy. No single app, VPN, browser, or setting makes someone completely anonymous or untraceable. Use privacy tools responsibly and follow the rules of devices and networks you use. Start by deciding what information you want to protect and from whom.

1. DNS and encrypted DNS

DNS translates a domain name into the network address a device needs to connect. Traditional DNS is often unencrypted between your device and resolver. DNS over HTTPS (DoH) and DNS over TLS (DoT) encrypt that connection to the resolver, but the resolver itself can still process the queries.

Safe setup checklist

  1. Choose a resolver whose privacy policy and operator you understand.
  2. Use your browser or operating system's built-in encrypted-DNS settings where available.
  3. Avoid installing random DNS certificates or configuration profiles.
  4. Check that your browser is actually using the intended resolver after setup.
  5. Remember that managed networks may enforce their own DNS settings.

Cloudflare: configure DoH in common browsers · Cloudflare: Linux setup guide · Cloudflare resolver check

Encrypted DNS protects DNS traffic on the path to the resolver. It does not hide every website connection or make browsing anonymous.

2. VPNs: setup and limitations

A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN provider. Websites generally see the VPN server's public IP instead of your network's public IP. Your VPN provider becomes an important party you must trust.

Before connecting

  • Download the official app from the provider's official website or your device's trusted app store.
  • Keep the app updated and use its default secure protocol unless you have a clear reason to change it.
  • Learn what its kill switch and DNS protection settings do; test them using the provider's documentation.
  • Check whether you are using a full-device VPN app or only a browser extension. They protect different traffic.
  • Do not assume a VPN hides your identity when you sign in to an account or share identifying details.

3. Tor Browser

Tor Browser routes browsing through the Tor network and includes privacy protections intended to reduce tracking and browser fingerprinting. It is not a magic invisibility cloak, and it does not protect every app on a device.

Safer use principles

  • Get Tor Browser only from the official Tor Project website and keep it updated.
  • Keep its built-in privacy settings and avoid adding extensions that make your browser more unique.
  • Use HTTPS websites and be cautious about opening downloaded documents in external applications.
  • Signing in to a personal account can identify you to that service even when using Tor.
  • Understand that websites may block Tor connections and that connection speed can vary.

4. Personal OPSEC (operational security)

OPSEC is the habit of thinking about what information could expose something you want to keep private. For everyday personal safety, use it to reduce accidental oversharing and protect accounts—not to promise that someone can become untraceable.

A simple threat-model worksheet

  1. Asset: What are you protecting? Examples include account access, private photos, location, or personal conversations.
  2. Threat: What could realistically go wrong? Examples include phishing, account takeover, doxxing, or a lost device.
  3. Exposure: Where could the information leak? Consider public profiles, app permissions, metadata, reused passwords, and shared devices.
  4. Controls: Use unique passwords, a password manager, multi-factor authentication, updates, backups, and careful sharing.
  5. Review: Revisit your choices when your device, accounts, or circumstances change.

Do not post private addresses, school or workplace details, real-time location, identity documents, recovery codes, or screenshots containing account tokens.

5. Browser privacy

  • Keep your browser and extensions updated.
  • Remove extensions you do not recognize or need.
  • Review site permissions for camera, microphone, location, and notifications.
  • Use built-in tracking protection and be cautious with third-party cookies.
  • Understand that browser fingerprinting can combine many ordinary settings into a recognizable pattern.

6. Password managers and multi-factor authentication

  • Use a different, strong password for every important account.
  • Store passwords in a reputable password manager and protect it with a strong master passphrase.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication or passkeys where supported.
  • Keep recovery codes somewhere private and secure; never share them with anyone.
  • Be alert for fake sign-in pages and unexpected password-reset messages.

7. Device encryption and backups

Device encryption helps protect stored files if a device is lost or accessed without authorization. Check your operating system's official documentation to understand whether encryption is enabled and where recovery keys are stored.

  • Keep recovery keys separate from the device they unlock.
  • Maintain backups of important files and test that you can restore them.
  • Use encrypted backups for sensitive material and protect backup credentials.
  • Keep the operating system and security software updated.

8. Metadata and safer sharing

Photos and documents can contain extra information such as timestamps, device details, author names, or location metadata. Screenshots can also reveal usernames, notifications, browser tabs, or account identifiers.

  • Review a file before posting it publicly.
  • Use built-in privacy or export settings to remove metadata when appropriate.
  • Crop or redact account details from screenshots before sharing.
  • Do not upload identity documents, private messages, or images that reveal your location.

9. Linux and network basics

Learn the purpose of DNS, IP addresses, routing, firewalls, TLS certificates, and software repositories before changing system settings. Prefer official distribution documentation and signed software repositories. Make a backup before changing network configuration.

  • Install security updates through your distribution's supported update mechanism.
  • Use trusted repositories and verify software sources.
  • Keep firewall rules understandable and document changes.
  • Do not paste commands from unknown sources into a root terminal.
